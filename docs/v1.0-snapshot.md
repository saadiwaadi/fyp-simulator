# v1.0 Snapshot: Probability Engine Baseline

## Date
April 2026

## Purpose of This Snapshot
This document captures the system at the point where the probability engine is stable and reproducible, while the spatial simulation layer has been implemented but is not yet fully coupled to outcome resolution. It serves as methodology evidence for iterative system development and controlled transition planning.

## What the System Does at This Point
The match engine runs a complete minute-by-minute simulation with:
- team tactical inputs (tempo, press, risk, depth, width) on a 1-5 scale,
- role-based player selection (GK, DEF, MID, FWD),
- possession, break, and finishing phases,
- structure degradation/regeneration dynamics,
- stamina and recovery effects,
- commentary and analytical report outputs.

The core outcome path is statistically driven:
- duelists are selected by role/zone weighting,
- phase success is resolved using player stats + tactical multipliers + random rolls,
- scoreline emerges from repeated probabilistic events.

## What Is Working
### 1) Probability Match Flow
- End-to-end matches run reliably.
- Tactical sliders influence event probabilities and tempo of state changes.
- Structural integrity model affects progression and shot quality pressure.

### 2) Spatial and Movement Layer Built
- Continuous player coordinates (x, y) and velocity (vx, vy) are present.
- Steering behaviors exist (arrive, velocity application).
- Formation targeting exists with:
  - role anchors,
  - tactical style transforms,
  - trait-based resistance/compliance,
  - deterministic same-role width spreading.
- Field dimensions and boundary clamping are active in movement target generation.

### 3) Traceability and Testing
- Stress and Monte Carlo scripts produce repeatable scenario summaries.
- The architecture is now modularized into core, entities, systems, and AI helpers.

## What Is Disconnected (Critical Limitation)
The largest limitation at this stage is coupling:
- Player movement and positioning run each minute,
- but most outcome resolution remains primarily statistical and role-weight based.

In practical terms:
- duel selection is still mostly role/zone probability, not nearest-space geometry,
- possession/break/finish mechanics do not yet consume spatial distances/angles/interception paths,
- influence-map and ball-tracking helpers exist but are not yet fully integrated in the live outcome path.

This means spatial behavior is visible, but only partially causal for scoring outcomes.

## Why Transition to Spatially-Coupled Simulation
Transition is made to address realism and explanatory validity:
1. Physical Plausibility
- Match events should depend on where players actually are, not only their role and base stats.

2. Tactical Interpretability
- Manager instructions (press/depth/width) should produce measurable positional consequences that directly alter duel likelihood and chance creation.

3. Trait Expression Fidelity
- Discipline, risk appetite, work rate, and tactical fit should affect spacing, recovery, and contest timing in a way that is causally linked to outcomes.

4. Research Credibility
- For FYP methodology, explicit acknowledgement of model limits and staged coupling demonstrates scientific rigor.

## Known Issues at This Snapshot
### 1) Scoring Volatility
- Output goals per match remain above target realism in several scenarios.
- Variance and high-goal frequency indicate over-permissive chance conversion and/or phase success stacking.

### 2) Spatial Layer Not Fully Linked to Outcomes
- Positioning logic influences movement trajectories,
- but not yet fully integrated into duelist selection and interception resolution.

### 3) Potential Double-Counting of Tactical Effects
- Tactical sliders affect both spatial targeting and probability modifiers.
- Without calibration, this can amplify effects and inflate instability.

## Next Integration Priorities After This Snapshot
1. Make duelist selection position-aware (distance and reachable zone constraints).
2. Add interception checks using ball trajectory + player reach.
3. Feed positional context into possession and break difficulty.
4. Recalibrate finishing and chance generation to reduce scoring inflation.
5. Validate scenario outputs against target behavioral bands.
